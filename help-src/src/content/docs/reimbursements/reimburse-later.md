An HSA is triple tax-advantaged: contributions go in tax-free, investments grow tax-free, and withdrawals for qualified medical expenses come out tax-free.

There's no deadline to pay yourself back for an eligible expense, as long as it happened after you opened your HSA. So instead of withdrawing right away, many people:

1. Pay medical bills out of pocket.
2. Leave the money in their HSA, invested, to grow for years or decades.
3. Reimburse themselves later, tax-free, whenever they need the funds.

This requires keeping records of every expense for years. HSA Monster keeps those records organized and ready.

## Your Reimbursable total

**Reimbursable** on the [Summary screen](/help/getting-started/summary/) is the money you can still withdraw tax-free:

**Out-of-pocket expenses − reimbursements you've taken = Reimbursable**

Expenses you paid directly from your HSA don't count, since your HSA already covered them.

## First In, First Out

Reimbursements come out of the total, not out of specific expenses, so you never have to match them up. Reports apply them to your oldest expenses first.

For example, if you log 10 expenses for $100 each, HSA Monster will display a reimbursable balance of $1,000. Should you reimburse yourself $250, HSA Monster will show the first two expenses and half of the third as reimbursed when you create a **Reimbursement Report**. Your summary will show $750 remaining in your reimbursable balance.

## Keep your receipts

If the IRS asks, you'll need a receipt for every expense you reimburse. Attach them as you go, and turn on **Settings → Exclude Missing Receipts** so your total only counts expenses you can back up.

:::note
HSA Monster is a record-keeping tool, not tax advice. Check IRS Publication 969 or a tax professional for your situation.
:::
